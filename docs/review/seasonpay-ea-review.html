SeasonPay · Rules review for an Enrolled Agent

Generated 2026-10-30 · 90 rules · 84 pending review
What SeasonPay is. A free, offline-first web app (pilot, winter season Dec 2026 to Mar 2027) for seasonal workers in the US on J-1 and H-2B visas. Users log shifts, tips and paystubs; the app estimates pay and overtime, flags possible Social Security/Medicare (FICA) withholding for nonresident J-1 workers, lists filing deadlines and an end-of-season checklist.

What it does not do. It does not prepare or file returns, does not compute income tax owed or refunds, and does not apply tax treaties (the pilot group is mostly from Peru, which has no income tax treaty with the US). Every message is phrased as information with a link to the official source and the date it was verified ("According to the IRS…, you may… Confirm with your employer, sponsor or a professional").

How rules work. No tax or labor figure is hardcoded. Each rule is a row in tax_rules (key, value, year, jurisdiction, source URL, verified date, reviewer). The app shows the source link and verified date next to every rule.
What we ask you to review.
  • Section 1 (federal) and the user-facing texts in Section 5: accuracy and wording. This is the priority.
  • Section 2 (state returns): whether the summaries are correct for nonresident seasonal workers.
  • Sections 3 and 4 (overtime): labor-law data from the US Department of Labor table; a quick sanity check is enough.
Please tick OK or Needs change and write notes. If you approve a rule, we will set reviewed_by to your name and the date.
Known limitations (by design in the pilot).
  • Federal rules use Publication 519 (2025) and the 2025 forms, the latest published as of 2026-10-30. They will be re-verified when the 2026 editions are released.
  • Deadlines: if a date falls on a weekend, the app shows the original date, flags it and explains the next-business-day rule (Pub. 519). It does not compute legal holidays (e.g., DC Emancipation Day).
  • Overtime: the app applies weekly overtime, daily overtime in AK, CA and CO, CA double time (over 12 h/day and over 8 h on the 7th consecutive day), and the CA and KY seventh-day rules (KY only when the week exceeds 40 h, per KRS 337.050). Each shift counts on the day it starts, so a long overnight shift covers CO's 12 consecutive hours; two back-to-back shifts logged on different days are not merged. NV daily overtime depends on wage and is set by the user.
  • FICA: exemption logic covers nonresident F/J/M/Q under Pub. 519. It does not handle the student FICA exception under IRC 3121(b)(10).
  • Alaska: no explicit official page found stating there is no individual income tax; no state rule is shown for AK.
